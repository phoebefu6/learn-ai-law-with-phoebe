Part 1 · covers legal-analysis structure and the research-to-memo flow

Structure the analysis 6 min live

AI is good at scaffolding legal reasoning into the forms lawyers already use - IRAC or CREAC - and drafting a memo or brief from research you have grounded. It gives you a clean skeleton and a fast first draft to react to. What it does not give you is the reasoning or the authority: you own the analysis, and you verify every cite before it goes in.

LiveIRAC on demand - what AI structures well3 min▶

Structure is where AI earns its place in memo work. Give it the issue and your grounded research and it will lay out a clean IRAC or CREAC skeleton, draft each section, and keep the analysis organized - the scaffolding that a blank page makes slow. You then fill in the judgment and check the law.

  • Issue. A crisp statement of the legal question - refine what the AI proposes so it frames exactly what you were asked.
  • Rule. The governing law, drawn from research you grounded - every authority here gets verified before it stays.
  • Application. The reasoning that applies rule to facts. This is your analysis; the AI's draft is a starting point to sharpen, not accept.
  • Conclusion. The answer and its limits. You own the bottom line the reader will act on.
Real world

The blank page beaten in a minute. An associate with three grounded cases and a Friday deadline gave the AI the issue and the research and asked for an IRAC skeleton. In a minute she had a structured draft to react to instead of a blank screen - the hard part, the application section, now had a shape to argue with. She spent her hours sharpening the analysis and checking every cite, not fighting the outline. The draft saved the setup, not the thinking.

Self-studyGround first, draft second - the tie to b22 min read▶

The order matters. AI drafts well from research you have already grounded and verified; it fabricates when asked to supply the law itself. So the memo workflow is: ground the research (b2), verify the authorities, then let AI structure and draft from that verified material - never the reverse.

  • Feed it verified authorities. Paste the research you have grounded and confirmed. Do not ask the AI to find the cases - that is where the fabrication lives.
  • Watch for smuggled cites. Even drafting from your material, an AI can add an authority you did not give it. Every cite in the output gets checked, including ones you think you recognize.
  • The draft is 70%. Structure and prose come fast; the reasoning, the judgment, and the verification are the 30% that is the actual lawyering.
Part 2 · covers the citation-verification duty in written work product

Citation discipline 5 min live

Every authority in a memo or brief is a promise to the reader - and to the court - that it exists, says what you claim, and is still good law. This is the b2 verification duty applied to written work product: no AI-supplied cite ships unverified. The 2023 sanctions were exactly this failure, in a filing.

LiveThe three checks every cite has to pass3 min▶

For every citation in the draft - whether the AI supplied it or you did - run the same three checks before it stays in the document. If a cite fails any one of them, it comes out. There is no volume exception and no deadline exception.

The checkWhat you confirm
It existsThe case, statute, or source is real - found in a genuine reporter or database, not a plausible invention
It holds what you claimYou read the actual opinion and it stands for the proposition the memo uses it for - not a miscited holding
It is still good lawNot overruled, reversed, superseded, or distinguished away for your jurisdiction and question
The pin cite is rightThe quoted language and pinpoint page actually appear where the citation says they do
It fits the jurisdictionThe authority is binding or persuasive for the court and question at hand, not a mismatch

The first three are the core test from b2. The last two are what a written filing adds. All five are yours to confirm before the memo leaves your desk.

Self-studyWhy a filing raises the stakes2 min read▶

In research, an unverified cite is a private mistake you can still catch. In a memo it misleads a colleague; in a brief it goes to a court under your signature, where candor to the tribunal (Model Rule 3.3) applies and a fabricated citation is sanctionable. Written work product is where the verification duty has teeth.

  • Candor to the tribunal. A brief is a representation to the court. "The AI generated it" has never been accepted as a defence.
  • A memo becomes reliance. A partner or client acts on your memo. A wrong or invented cite propagates into decisions and later filings.
  • Verification is the signature. Signing the document means you stand behind every authority in it. That is the professional act, not the drafting.
Part 3 · the mindset that makes the AI draft yours

Draft, then be the editor 4 min live

The AI gives you a structured first draft. Your job is not to accept it - it is to be its hardest editor: sharpen the analysis, check the law, cut the filler, and own the argument. The value you add moved from producing the draft to interrogating it.

LiveEditing is the lawyering now2 min▶

A first draft used to cost hours and prove effort. Now it is cheap, so the effort that counts shifts entirely to the edit: is the analysis actually right, is every authority real and on point, is the argument the strongest one available? That is where a lawyer's judgment lives, and it is exactly what AI cannot do for you.

  • Sharpen the analysis. AI drafts to the average. The distinctive argument, the best framing, the counterargument it missed - those are yours to add.
  • Check the law. Run the citation pass. Confirm the rule statement. Read the cases that carry weight. The draft's confidence is not evidence of its accuracy.
  • Own the argument. When the memo is done it reflects your reasoning and your name. There is no shared credit and no shared blame with the tool.
Real world

The draft that read well and argued weakly. An AI memo was fluent, organized, and made the obvious argument competently - and missed the stronger one that turned on a distinction in the facts. The associate who merely polished the prose would have filed a mediocre memo. The one who edited as a lawyer found the better argument the draft never reached. Same starting draft, entirely different work product. The edit was the value.

Say it out loud once "AI drafts, I verify every cite, I sharpen the analysis, I own the argument." That is the memo-and-brief discipline in a sentence.
Demo 1 of 2

Draft a short IRAC memo from grounded research ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Turn research you have grounded into a structured memo. Pick a non-confidential legal question, feed the AI your verified authorities, and get an IRAC draft to sharpen. This is research-to-memo, done safely.

Choose a non-confidential legal question and gather two or three authorities you have already grounded and verified (from b2, or supplied). Do not ask the AI to find the cases.

Paste the question and your verified authorities, and ask for a short IRAC memo: issue, rule, application, conclusion.

Read the draft as an editor. Is the issue framed right? Is the application actually your reasoning, or a generic one? What argument did it miss?

Sharpen it. Rewrite the application in your own analysis, add the counterargument, tighten the conclusion and its limits.

Mark every citation in the draft for the verification pass in demo 2 - including any the AI added that you did not supply.

★ The IRAC-memo prompt (grounded research only)Draft a short legal memo in IRAC format on this question: [non-confidential question]. Use ONLY the authorities I paste below - I have grounded and verified them. Do not add, find, or invent any case, statute, or citation I have not given you. If you think an authority is missing, say so in a note - do not supply it. Structure: Issue, Rule (from my authorities), Application, Conclusion. Keep the application tight and flag any place where my reasoning needs to go further. Plain, precise prose. Authorities: [paste your verified research here]
The draft is the setup, not the memo A clean IRAC skeleton in a minute is real leverage - but it is the starting line. The memo becomes yours in the edit: the application in your words, the counterargument it missed, and every cite verified. Ship the AI draft as-is and you have shipped the average.
Demo 2 of 2

Run a citation-verification pass on the memo ★ 8 min · build your own

Take the memo from demo 1 and put every authority in it through the three checks. This is the pass that keeps you out of the 2023 headlines - and it is non-negotiable before any memo or brief ships.

List every citation in the memo - cases, statutes, quotes, pin cites. Include any the AI slipped in that you did not supply.

For each one, confirm it exists in a real reporter or database. Any you cannot find in a genuine source comes out immediately.

Confirm it holds what you claim. Open the actual opinion and read the proposition the memo relies on it for - not the AI's summary of it.

Confirm it is still good law for your jurisdiction and question - not overruled, reversed, or distinguished away. Check the pin cite and quote too.

Only cites that pass all three stay. The verified memo - not the first draft - is the work product you sign and stand behind.

★ The citation-verification checklist promptList every legal authority cited in the memo below - cases, statutes, quotes, and pin cites - in a table. For each, leave three empty columns for me to fill by hand: 1) Exists? (found in a real reporter/database) 2) Holds what the memo claims? (I read the opinion) 3) Still good law + right jurisdiction? Do not tell me whether they are valid - I verify each one myself against primary sources. Flag any citation you are unsure you generated correctly so I check it first.
Real world

The pass that caught the smuggled cite. A verification pass on an otherwise solid brief turned up one citation the drafter did not remember adding - the AI had introduced it while tightening a paragraph. The case was real but stood for the opposite of what the sentence claimed. Ten minutes of checking removed a cite that would have handed opposing counsel the argument. The draft was 95% right; the verification found the 5% that mattered.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Draft a full IRAC memo from grounded research on a non-confidential question. Edit the application in your own words and add the strongest counterargument the AI missed.
  • Run the citation-verification pass on every authority - the three checks plus pin cite and jurisdiction. Count how many needed a fix.
  • Note any citation the AI added that you did not supply. That is the smuggled-cite risk, felt first-hand.
  • Write your one-line rule for written work product: no AI-supplied cite ships until it passes all three checks. Pin it above your desk.
  • Optional: turn the memo into a short brief section and re-run the verification pass under the candor-to-the-tribunal standard. Bring one question to b7.
