Part 1 · covers the agentic + pricing outlook (◐ re-verify)

The agentic legal future, realistically 12 min live

Agentic legal workflows - AI that chains multiple steps of research, drafting, and review with a human signing off at the end - are genuinely emerging. The honest split for the next 24 months: grounded, supervised augmentation is real and getting better; autonomous lawyering is not. Alongside it, the billable hour comes under pressure, because value shifts from hours spent to judgment and verification applied. This outlook moves fast, so re-verify it before you brief your board.

LiveReal in 24 months vs hype4 min▶

Give partners a clean line between what to plan for and what to discount. Both matter: over-promising autonomy erodes trust and invites the sanctions you spent session five avoiding.

  • Real: grounded, supervised augmentation. AI drafts, researches, and reviews across multiple steps; a licensed lawyer verifies and owns the result. This gets steadily better and more workflow-like.
  • Real: agentic workflows under governance - multi-step research and drafting with a human sign-off gate - reaching pilots and controlled use.
  • Hype: autonomous lawyering. AI that advises clients or files work without a lawyer in the loop is not the 24-month picture, and treating it as such is a liability.
  • The constant: more capability raises the stakes on verification, it does not retire it. The more the AI does, the more there is to check.
Real world

Augmentation is the near future; autonomy is the pitch deck. The tools genuinely compressing legal work today still put a lawyer at the sign-off gate - that is what makes them defensible. The vendors promising the lawyer out of the loop are selling a future that the professional-responsibility rules, and the sanctions record, are not ready to support. Plan for the first; be skeptical of the second.

Questions to ask your team Where in our practice would supervised augmentation help most in the next year? Which vendor claims are we taking at face value that we should re-verify? Are we planning for augmentation or quietly betting on autonomy?
LiveThe billable hour and pricing3 min▶

When AI produces a first draft in seconds, hours-based pricing stops tracking value. The value did not disappear - it moved, from time spent to judgment and verification applied. That is a strategic conversation, not just an operational one, and leaders who name it early shape it rather than react to it.

  • Value shifts to judgment and verification. The scarce, billable skill is catching what is wrong and owning the result - not producing the draft.
  • Pricing models come under pressure. Expect more interest in value-based, fixed-fee, and outcome-linked arrangements as the hours behind a task compress.
  • Re-verify. Pricing trends are directional and move fast - treat this as a conversation to open, not a forecast to bank.
Questions to ask your team If a task that took eight hours now takes two, how do we price it - and how do we explain the value? What would a value-based version of our top matter type look like? Who owns the pricing-model conversation with clients?
Self-studyWhat "agentic" actually means here2 min read▶

Cut through the term. An agentic workflow is AI that takes a goal and executes several steps toward it - pull sources, draft, check against a playbook, revise - rather than answering one prompt at a time. In legal work the non-negotiable is a human sign-off gate at the end: the lawyer verifies and owns the output. Agentic does not mean autonomous. The strategic opportunity is workflows that compress many steps; the strategic risk is letting the sign-off gate quietly disappear because the chain got long and convenient.

Questions to ask your team When we say "agentic," do we all mean the same thing? Where is the human sign-off gate in the workflows we are piloting - and could it erode without anyone deciding to remove it?
Part 2 · covers the transformation sequence

Sequencing the transformation 10 min live

Three phases, in order, and the order is the whole point. Foundation before adoption, adoption before transformation. The firms that get sanctioned are the ones that reach for agentic workflows before they have built a verification culture. Do not skip the foundation - it is not the boring prelude, it is the thing that makes everything after it safe.

LiveFoundation, adoption, transformation4 min▶
PhaseWhat it builds
FoundationVerification culture, ethics policy, vetted tools, and training - the session-five stack made real
AdoptionAugmented research, drafting, and review, with a lawyer verifying and owning every output
TransformationGoverned agentic workflows - multi-step, with the human sign-off gate held firmly in place

Each phase depends on the one before it. Adoption without a verification culture is how unverified output reaches a court; transformation without governed adoption is how a long agentic chain quietly loses its sign-off gate. Sequence it, and each step makes the next one safe.

Questions to ask your team Honestly, which phase are we in - not which one we would like to claim? Are we tempted to skip foundation because it feels slow? What is the one thing that has to be true before we move to the next phase?
LiveWhy skipping the foundation gets firms sanctioned3 min▶

The sanctions cases are not stories about advanced AI gone wrong - they are stories about adoption without foundation. A lawyer reached for a capable tool before a verification culture existed to catch its confident fiction. The lesson for a leader planning transformation is direct: the more capable the AI you adopt, the more essential the foundation you built first. Speed at the front of the roadmap is bought with discipline at the base of it.

Not legal advice Sequencing guidance is strategic, not compliance. Your jurisdiction's rules, court expectations, and carrier requirements govern what "adequate foundation" means for your firm - confirm with counsel who knows your bar before you scale.
Part 3 · the story you take to partners

The partnership narrative 12 min live

A roadmap does not fund itself; a narrative does. The story that moves partners has four beats: the skills-gap threat and the opportunity inside it, verification as both the moat and the duty, the guardrails that make it safe, and a clear ask - investment and training, with milestones. Woven through it is the pricing-model conversation, because that is the strategic question partners will ask first.

LiveThe four beats of the narrative4 min▶

Tell it in this order. Each beat answers the objection the previous one raises.

  • Threat and opportunity. Around 80% of firms are investing in AI while only about a third feel their lawyers are ready. That gap is both the risk of being left behind and the opening to lead - name both.
  • Verification as moat and duty. The firm's edge is not the AI everyone will have; it is the discipline to verify and own the work. That discipline is also the professional duty, which makes it defensible as well as differentiating.
  • The guardrails. Show the governance from session five - the policy, the verification rule, the confidentiality lines. This is what lets partners say yes without fearing the headline.
  • The ask and milestones. Be specific: investment in vetted tools and training, on a phased roadmap with milestones partners can hold you to. Vague asks get deferred.
Questions to ask your team What is our honest version of the skills-gap number, and does it land as threat or opportunity here? Can we state our moat in one sentence a partner would repeat? Is the ask specific enough to approve, or vague enough to defer?
LiveThe pricing-model conversation3 min▶

Partners will connect AI to revenue faster than to risk, so meet them there. If AI compresses the hours behind a matter, the billable-hour model comes under pressure and the value shifts to judgment and verification. Frame this as a conversation to lead rather than a threat to fear: the firms that redesign pricing around the value they actually add will do better than those defending hours that AI has already compressed. Keep it directional and re-verify the trend before you commit.

Real world

The moat is the boring part. When every firm has the same models, the differentiator is not the tool - it is the verification culture, the governance, and the judgment behind the sign-off. The firm that treats verification as its brand, not its overhead, is the one clients trust with the work that matters. Lead with that, and the investment ask answers itself.

Questions to ask your team Which of our matter types is most exposed to hour compression, and what would value-based pricing look like there? Are we leading the pricing conversation with clients or waiting for them to raise it?
Self-studyThe skills gap as a talent story2 min read▶

The survey number - roughly 80% of firms investing while only about a third feel their lawyers are ready - is usually told as a technology gap. For partners it lands harder as a talent story. The lawyers who learn to use AI well and verify rigorously become more valuable, not less; the ones who do not risk being outpaced. A roadmap that funds training is also a retention and recruiting argument: the firm that builds AI-and-verification skill is the one ambitious associates want to join and the one clients trust with compressed, higher-stakes work. Fold that into the narrative and the training ask stops being a cost line and becomes a talent strategy.

Questions to ask your team Do our best associates see us as ahead or behind on AI - and what does that do to retention? Is our training ask framed as a cost or as a talent investment? Who, specifically, gets more valuable if this roadmap works?
Leader exercise

Sketch your 24-month roadmap & narrative beats ◐ 15 min · with your team

The finale deliverable: a roadmap you could put on one slide, and the story that funds it.

  • Draw the strip. Sketch now, 6, 12, and 24 months. Place your firm honestly on it - most leaders are earlier than they think. Mark what has to be true to move from each stage to the next.
  • Anchor the foundation. Write the one foundation item you will not skip - the verification culture or ethics policy that everything after it depends on. This is the non-negotiable.
  • Write the four beats. One sentence each: threat and opportunity, verification as moat and duty, the guardrails, and the ask with milestones. This is your partnership narrative in four lines.
  • Name the ask. Be specific about investment and training, and attach at least one milestone a partner could hold you to. A narrative with no ask is a briefing, not a strategy.
  • Add the pricing line. Draft the one sentence you will use to open the pricing-model conversation with partners.
Questions to ask your team If we did nothing for 24 months, what would it cost us - and can we say that in one sentence? What is the smallest credible first milestone that proves the roadmap is real? Who in the partnership has to champion this for it to move?
