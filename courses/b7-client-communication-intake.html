Part 1 · covers Research.com client comms - plain-language translation

Turn dense law into plain language 6 min live

A client does not need the analysis - they need to know what it means for them and what happens next. AI is genuinely good at rewriting complex reasoning into clear, calm language and at streamlining intake questionnaires. What it is not is the author of the advice: the lawyer reviews every client-facing communication and owns what it says before it is sent.

LiveTranslation is the skill - the review is the duty3 min▶

The hard part of client communication is not the law - you already did that. It is saying it so a non-lawyer understands their position, their options, and the next step, without losing accuracy or overpromising. AI is a strong first translator: feed it your analysis and it returns a clear draft. Then you read it as the author, because the moment it goes out, it is your advice.

  • Clarity, not dumbing down. Plain language keeps the meaning and drops the jargon. AI is good at this; you confirm nothing was lost or softened into a wrong impression.
  • No new promises. An AI rewrite can quietly add certainty you never gave - "you will win" where you said "you have a strong argument". Catch that on review.
  • The lawyer owns the advice. A client-facing update is legal communication. You review and own every one - the AI drafted it, but it speaks for you.
★ Try it now (over non-confidential text) - the plain-language askRewrite this legal analysis as a short client update in plain language a non-lawyer can follow. Keep the meaning exact. Do not add certainty I did not state. End with clear next steps. Flag anything you were unsure how to phrase so I can check it.
Real world

The update the client actually read. A three-page memo became a six-line email: here is where you stand, here are your two options, here is what we recommend and why, here is the next step. The client replied in an hour instead of calling confused a week later. The lawyer wrote none of the first draft and every bit of the judgment in it - and read every line before it sent.

Self-studyGood plain-language client comms - a quick checklist2 min read▶
  • Lead with the answer - where the client stands, in one line, before the reasoning.
  • Options, then recommendation - lay out the choices, then say what you advise and why.
  • Name the next step - what happens now, who does it, by when.
  • No unstated certainty - "strong argument" is not "guaranteed win". Keep the hedge you meant.
  • You review before it sends - every client-facing draft, no exceptions. The advice is yours.
Part 2 · covers Research.com client comms - intake + efficiency

Intake + efficiency 5 min live

Intake is repetitive, structured, and slow - exactly what AI helps with. It can draft an intake questionnaire, surface issues from what a prospective client describes (as leads to investigate, never conclusions), and produce a first-response template. The guardrail: confidentiality of everything a prospective or actual client tells you, and a lawyer between the AI and any answer.

AI can shorten every step of intake without ever making the call. Each task below pairs with a check the lawyer keeps.

Issue-spotting is a lead, not a diagnosis When AI reads an intake and says "this looks like a limitations problem", that is a prompt for you to check the limitation period - not a finding to relay to the client. The lawyer confirms every issue before it becomes advice.
Self-studyConfidentiality starts at hello2 min read▶
  • Prospective-client duties apply early. Information a would-be client shares can carry confidentiality obligations before they ever become a client. Treat intake data as sensitive from the first message.
  • Vetted tools only. If intake content goes near AI, it goes into a tool with confidentiality terms suited to client data - never a consumer chatbot.
  • Conflicts before content. Intake is also where conflicts surface. AI can help organise the check; the lawyer runs and clears it.
  • First response is not advice. A template acknowledging an inquiry is fine; a template that answers the legal question is not - route that to a lawyer.
Part 3 · the loudest rail of the track

The UPL + advice line - read this twice 5 min live

This is the rail that matters most the instant AI faces a client. AI output is not legal advice. Never let an AI-generated communication stand as advice to a client - and never, ever to a non-client - without a lawyer's review. A lawyer reviews and owns every client-facing legal communication. No exceptions, no "it was just a draft".

LiveWhy the client-facing line is the sharpest one3 min▶

Internally, an unverified AI draft is a mistake you can catch. Facing a client, it is advice the moment they read it - and they act on it. If it reaches a non-client, it can be unauthorized practice of law and the giving of legal advice by a machine no one is standing behind. That is why this rail is louder here than anywhere else in the course.

  • AI does not give legal advice. It generates text. Advice requires a lawyer's judgment applied to a specific client's facts - and a lawyer accountable for it.
  • A client acts on what you send. "The AI drafted it" is no comfort to a client who relied on a wrong answer, and no defence to you.
  • A non-client is the bright red line. Letting AI answer legal questions for people who are not your clients, with no lawyer reviewing, is unauthorized practice - the exact thing UPL rules exist to stop.
  • The lawyer owns every client-facing word. Review, correct, and own it before it is sent. That ownership is the whole point of the rail.
Say it out loud once "AI is not legal advice. A lawyer reviews and owns every client-facing communication - to a client, and never to a non-client without me." That sentence is the spine of this session.
Self-studyWhat the rail forbids, plainly2 min read▶
  • Never let an AI-generated answer go to a client as advice without a lawyer reading and owning it.
  • Never deploy an AI tool that answers legal questions for non-clients on its own - that is unauthorized practice of law.
  • Never present an AI plain-language rewrite as final without confirming it did not add certainty or drop a caveat.
  • Always keep a lawyer-review gate between any AI draft and any client-facing send.
  • Always remember this is education for legal professionals, not legal advice; your bar's rules control.
Demo 1 of 2

Dense analysis to a plain-language client update ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Take a (non-confidential, synthetic) legal analysis and turn it into a client update anyone can follow - then review and own it as the lawyer. This is rail one and the plain-language skill in one build.

Start from a short non-confidential or synthetic legal analysis - a paragraph or two of real legal reasoning with a conclusion. Nothing from a live client matter.

Ask the AI for a plain-language client update: lead with where the client stands, then options, then your recommendation and the next step.

Read it as the author. Check three things: is the meaning intact, did it add certainty you never gave, and did any caveat quietly disappear?

Edit hard. Restore the hedge you meant, fix the tone, add the specific next step. The AI draft is the scaffold; the judgment is yours.

Mark it "reviewed and owned by [lawyer]" in your process. That gate is what makes it safe to send.

The rewrite to distrust (added certainty)Good news - you are going to win this. There is nothing to worry about and the other side has no case, so we can proceed with full confidence. [The analysis said "a strong but not guaranteed argument" - the AI upgraded it.]

Demo 2 of 2

An intake questionnaire + first-response template ★ 8 min · build your own

A reusable pair you will keep: an intake questionnaire for a matter type and a first-response template - both with a lawyer-review gate written into them so no answer goes out un-reviewed.

Pick a common matter type in your practice. Ask the AI to draft an intake questionnaire that captures the facts you need before a first consultation.

Review the questions. Add what it missed, cut what invites privileged detail too early, and confirm nothing there gives advice.

Ask for a first-response template that acknowledges the inquiry, sets expectations, and routes the legal questions to a lawyer - it must not answer them.

Write the gate in explicitly: "a lawyer reviews and owns any substantive response before it is sent." Keep it at the top where no one can miss it.

★ The intake + first-response promptDraft two things for a legal intake process on [matter type]: 1) An intake questionnaire capturing the facts we need before a first consultation. Do not ask for privileged detail before we have cleared conflicts. 2) A first-response template that acknowledges the inquiry and sets expectations. It must NOT answer the legal question - route substantive questions to a lawyer. Put a line at the top of both: "A lawyer reviews and owns any substantive response before it is sent. AI output is not legal advice." Plain language.
Real world

The intake that never gave advice by accident. A solo practice let AI draft its intake flow and, at first, the auto-response cheerfully answered the legal question. One edit fixed it: the template now acknowledges, sets expectations, and hands every substantive question to the lawyer. Faster intake, zero unauthorized advice - because the gate was written in, not assumed.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Finish the plain-language client update if you did not complete it live. Compare your reviewed version to the raw AI draft - note what you had to fix.
  • Build one intake questionnaire and one first-response template for a matter type you handle, each with the lawyer-review gate written in at the top.
  • Take one real (redacted, non-confidential) analysis you have on file and produce a plain-language version. Time how long the whole cycle takes.
  • Check your first-response template does not answer a legal question anywhere. If it does, route it to a lawyer instead.
  • Optional: draft a one-line house rule - "a lawyer reviews and owns every client-facing communication" - and put it where your team drafts client messages.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What is AI genuinely good at in client communication?

AI is a strong first translator - it turns your analysis into clear language fast. It does not decide the advice or send it. You review, correct, and own the update before it reaches the client.

2 · An AI drafts a warm, clear reply that answers a client's legal question. What do you do before it goes out?

A client-facing legal communication is advice the instant the client acts on it. The lawyer reviews and owns every one. "The AI drafted it" is no defence, and a disclaimer does not transfer the duty.

3 · Where is the line the sharpest?

The UPL rail is loudest facing non-clients. AI output is not legal advice; letting it answer legal questions for people who are not your clients, unreviewed, is unauthorized practice of law. A lawyer must review and own it.
