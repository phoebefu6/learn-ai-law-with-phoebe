Part 1 · covers GC AI use-case guidance + risk canon

Value vs risk: the scoring grid 6 min live

Two axes. Value is how much time and leverage a use-case creates for your lawyers. Risk is the chance and cost of it going wrong - and in legal work that means four specific things: hallucinated law and citations, privilege and confidentiality exposure, unauthorized practice of law, and the malpractice tail behind all of them. Plot each use-case and the shape of your strategy becomes obvious: start high-value and low-risk, and keep the two "avoid" corners off the board entirely.

LiveReading the grid - four zones, one order3 min▶

The grid is not a scoreboard, it is a sequence. Where a use-case lands tells you not just whether to do it but when.

  • Quick wins (high value, low risk). Summarizing documents your lawyers supply, drafting from a playbook over non-confidential text. Real leverage, contained downside, a human still reviews. Start here.
  • Verify-heavy (high value, high risk). Legal research is the classic case - hugely valuable, and the exact place AI fabricates. Do it, but only once the verification habit is built and grounded tools are in place.
  • Low priority (low value, low risk). Fine to let lawyers experiment, not worth a program.
  • Avoid. Client-confidential data in a consumer tool (privilege), and AI as legal advice (UPL). These stay off the board regardless of how much time they might save.
Real world

The tempting bet in the wrong corner. The use-case leaders most want to start with is often research - it is visible, valuable, and demos beautifully. It is also the single highest-risk square on the board, because it is exactly where AI invents cases. Teams that start there before building the verification reflex are placing their biggest bet on their least-ready muscle. Start with summarizing and drafting; earn the research bet.

★ Questions to ask your team Which use-case are we most tempted to start with, and which zone does it actually sit in? Is anything we already do quietly in the avoid corner? What would move a verify-heavy bet into "ready"?

Part 3 · the order that earns trust

Sequencing: earn trust before you climb 5 min live

The grid tells you where to start; sequencing tells you how to move. The rule is simple and it protects you: start in the quick-win corner, use those wins to build the verification habit across your lawyers, then climb into research and diligence with grounding and vetted tools. Never let client-confidential data touch a consumer tool, at any step. Trust is earned rung by rung - it is not granted by a vendor demo.

LiveThe climb - and the line that never moves3 min▶

Sequencing is where a2 connects back to the three eras from a1. Each step up the grid is a step up the eras, and each one is earned.

  • Step 1 - start in the quick-win corner. Drafting and summarizing over supplied, non-confidential text, with a human review every time. This is where the verification habit forms cheaply.
  • Step 2 - build the habit into muscle. Make "verify every cite, own the output" the norm on low-risk work before it has to carry high-risk work.
  • Step 3 - climb to research and diligence. Only with grounded, vetted tools and the habit already in place. This is where the biggest value sits and the biggest risk lives.
  • The line that never moves. No client-confidential or privileged data in a consumer tool, ever, at any step. That is not a sequencing choice - it is rail two.
Real world

Why the boring first bet is the smart one. A function that spends its first quarter just letting lawyers summarize supplied documents looks unambitious next to one chasing autonomous research. But the first has quietly built the reflex - verify, own it - that makes the research bet survivable, while the second has trained its people to trust output at exactly the wrong altitude. The unglamorous corner is where trust is manufactured.

★ Questions to ask your team Are we trying to climb before the habit is built? What would prove the verification habit is real - not just written in a policy? Is our confidentiality line actually holding at every step, or only in principle?
Self-studyWhat "ready to climb" actually looks like2 min read▶

Leaders ask when they are allowed to move up. The honest answer is behavioural, not calendar-based - you climb when the evidence says the habit holds, not when a quarter ends.

  • Verification is observed, not assumed. Lawyers are actually pulling and reading cited cases, and you can point to it, not just to a policy.
  • Vetted tools are in place. For any higher-risk step, grounded and confidential legal tools are chosen and approved - a4 covers this.
  • Accountability is clear. Everyone knows who owns the output when AI is in the loop - a5 makes this explicit.
  • The confidentiality line is not being tested. No quiet workarounds pasting client data into consumer tools "just this once".
★ Questions to ask your team Can we show evidence the verification habit is real? Have we approved the tools the next step needs? Who would be accountable if the next bet went wrong tomorrow?
Leader exercise

Score five real use-cases and pick your first bet ◐ 20-30 min · decide tonight

Take the map you brought from a1 and turn it into a scored shortlist. The output is one decision: your first bet, and why it is defensible.

  • Pick five real use-cases from your practice - use the inventory as a starting menu.
  • Score each on value (time saved, leverage) and on the four risks (hallucination, privilege, UPL, malpractice tail). One to five is enough.
  • Plot them on the grid. Circle anything that lands in the avoid corner and take it off the board now.
  • Choose your first bet from the quick-win corner - high value, low risk, human-reviewed - and write one sentence on why it is the right place to start.
  • Name the one condition that would let you climb to a verify-heavy bet next. Carry both into a3, where the ethics behind the risk axis get their full hour.
★ Questions to ask your team Do we all agree on the first bet, or is someone quietly reaching for the research square? What is the single condition standing between us and the next bet? Which use-case did we score as "safe" that deserves a second look?

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Which is the strongest FIRST bet for a legal function new to AI?

Start in the quick-win corner: high value, low risk, human-reviewed. Summarizing supplied docs builds the verification habit cheaply. Research is the highest-risk square (hallucination) and a self-serve advice tool edges into UPL - both are bets you earn, not start with.

2 · Why does the grid put risk on its own axis instead of folding it into value?

A high-value use-case can be dangerous on any of four separate risks. Scoring risk explicitly - hallucination, privilege / confidentiality, UPL, and the malpractice tail - stops a valuable-but-risky bet from hiding behind its upside.

3 · What is the correct sequencing rule?

Earn trust before you climb: quick wins first, build the verify-and-own habit, then move to verify-heavy work with grounding and vetted tools. The confidentiality line - no client-confidential data in consumer tools - never moves at any step.
