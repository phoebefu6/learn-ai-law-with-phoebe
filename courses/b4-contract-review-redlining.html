Contract review & redlining: AI flags, the lawyer decides

Reviewing a contract against your playbook is exactly the kind of patient, comparison-heavy work AI is good at - and exactly the kind where judgment cannot be delegated. Tonight you learn to have AI compare an agreement to YOUR playbook, flag deviations, risks, and missing terms, and draft redlines to your fallback positions - while you adjudicate each flag. Faster triage, same lawyer's call on what matters.

Part 0

Why review is a decision, not a scan

In b3 you drafted from your playbook. Review is the mirror image: instead of writing to your standards, you measure someone else's contract against them. AI is strong here - it compares tirelessly, spots the clause that is missing, and drafts a redline in seconds. But flagging is not deciding. The model can tell you a liability cap deviates from your fallback; it cannot tell you whether this deviation is worth fighting over on this deal. That call is yours, on every flag. A reminder before we start: this is education for legal professionals, not legal advice, and professional-conduct rules vary by jurisdiction - verify against your bar.

Part 1 · covers GC AI playbook review + Spellbook redlining

Playbook-based review 6 min live

A playbook review is a comparison: this contract, against your standards. Give AI both and it does the tedious part fast - flagging where the agreement deviates from your positions, where it carries risk, and where an expected term is simply missing. Then it can draft the redlines. Every arrow in this flow points forward, but one box in the middle is the whole point: the lawyer decides each flag.

LiveWhat AI flags well - and what it cannot judge3 min▶

Given the contract and your playbook, AI is genuinely good at surfacing what a tired human eye misses at midnight: the indemnity that runs one way, the cap that is missing, the termination notice that is thirty days short of your standard. What it cannot do is weigh those flags against the deal in front of you. A deviation that is fatal on one transaction is a rounding error on another. That weighing is the lawyer's work.

  • Flagging is comparison; deciding is judgment. "This deviates from your fallback" is a fact the model can find. "This deviation kills the deal" is a call only you can make.
  • Missing terms are the hardest to see and the easiest for AI to catch. A clause that is not there does not jump off the page - but AI comparing to your playbook notices the gap.
  • The model can be wrong too. It can miss a buried cross-reference or misread a defined term. Verify that the flags are right and that nothing was missed - your review sits on top of its review.
★ Try it now (vetted tool, sample contract) - flag against a playbookCompare this services agreement against our playbook below. Flag every clause that deviates from our standard, every material risk, and every term we expect that is missing. For each flag, give the clause, our standard, the deviation, and a risk level. Do not redline yet - just surface, so I can decide.
Real world

The missing clause the AI caught. A reviewer ran a vendor's "clean" agreement against the firm's playbook. The AI flagged what was not there: no limitation-of-liability clause at all, which under the governing law left the client exposed without a cap. A fast human read had skimmed past the absence - you notice bad clauses, not missing ones. The lawyer decided it was material, and the redline added the cap. AI found the gap; the lawyer judged it and fixed it.

Self-studyReview against a playbook vs review from scratch2 min read▶

The playbook is what turns AI review from a generic risk-scan into your review.

  • From scratch, the model flags what a median contract would worry about - useful, but not aligned to your client or your positions.
  • Against your playbook, it flags what deviates from what you would have drafted - your standards become the yardstick, and the flags are actionable.
  • The playbook you built in b3 is the same one you review with. Standard clauses, fallback positions, party notes - drafting and review share one source of truth.
  • Legal-specific tools help. Platforms like GC AI and Spellbook review against a loaded playbook and draft redlines - but the adjudication stays with you.
Part 2 · covers risk flags, triage, and redlining to fallbacks

Risk-spotting + redlining 5 min live

Most review time goes to the same handful of clauses, because that is where the risk lives. Knowing the standard flags lets you triage a stack of agreements by risk before you read a word closely - and lets you draft redlines straight to the fallback positions in your playbook.

LiveThe standard risk flags - and triaging a stack by risk3 min▶

Point AI at these areas first; they carry the most exposure across almost every commercial agreement.

Risk areaWhat to check against your playbook
LiabilityIs there a cap? Is it mutual? Is the number and the carve-out list within your fallback?
IndemnityWhich way does it run, how broad is the trigger, and is it capped or uncapped?
TerminationNotice period, termination for convenience, cure rights, and what survives?
IPOwnership of deliverables, license scope, and any assignment of background IP?
DataData protection, security obligations, breach notice, and cross-border terms?
Triage before you read Feed a stack of agreements and ask AI to rank them by risk against your playbook - which have uncapped liability, one-way indemnities, or missing data terms. Read the high-risk ones closely first. You still read them; AI just tells you where to point your attention, so the riskiest contract is not the one you review last at 2am.
Self-studyDrafting redlines to your fallback positions2 min read▶
  • Redline to the fallback, not the standard. On review you are negotiating - open at your standard where you can, but the redline the model drafts should land at the fallback line you will accept.
  • Keep the counterparty's structure where you can. A redline that rewrites their whole clause invites a fight; one that surgically pulls the term to your fallback moves faster.
  • Draft the fallback rationale too. Ask the model for a one-line reason for each redline - useful in the negotiation, and a check that the change is justified.
  • You finalize every redline. The model drafts the markup; you decide the wording, the ask, and the order you will concede in.
Part 3 · the judgment AI cannot supply

The human decides 4 min live

AI surfaces and drafts. Everything that follows a flag - is it material, is it worth negotiating, what do we accept - is judgment, and judgment is the lawyer's. And because the model reviews imperfectly, part of your judgment is checking that it did not miss or misread.

LiveMateriality, strategy, and verifying the review2 min▶

Three things stay with you on every flag.

  • Materiality. Not every deviation matters. You weigh each flag against the deal - value, leverage, relationship, risk appetite - and decide which are worth raising and which you let go.
  • Negotiation strategy. What you accept, what you trade, and the order you concede in is legal and commercial judgment. The model does not know your client's priorities; you do.
  • Verify the review itself. Confirm the flags are accurate and, harder, that nothing was missed. A missed cross-reference or a misread defined term is the model's failure that becomes your problem if you do not catch it. Your review sits on top of its review.
Say it out loud once "AI flags, I decide each - materiality, strategy, and whether it missed anything." That sentence is the spine of AI-assisted review. The tool makes you faster; it does not make the call.
Demo 1 of 2

Review a sample contract - adjudicate each flag ★ 13 min · everyone builds

The core skill, done once. Take a supplied sample contract and a supplied playbook, have AI flag every deviation and gap, then go flag by flag and decide each one. Use only the non-confidential sample material provided - no client data.

Open a vetted tool. Paste the sample contract and the supplied playbook (standard clauses + fallback positions + which party you act for).

Ask AI to flag every deviation from the playbook, every material risk, and every missing term - with the clause, your standard, the deviation, and a risk level for each. No redlines yet.

Go flag by flag. For each, decide: is this material on this deal? Accept, negotiate, or let go. Mark your call and a one-line reason.

Verify the review. Spot-check that the flags are right, and read the contract yourself for anything the model missed - especially a missing term or a buried cross-reference.

You now have a decided review: every flag adjudicated, nothing accepted blindly. That is the deliverable - AI flagged, you decided.

★ The playbook-review promptReview this [contract type] against our playbook below. For every clause that deviates from our standard, every material risk, and every expected term that is missing, give me: - the clause (or note it is missing) - our standard / fallback for it - how it deviates - a risk level (high / medium / low) We act for the [party]. Do not draft redlines yet - just surface, so I decide each. Then list anything you were unsure about or could not check. Playbook: [paste standard clauses + fallbacks].
Why this is demo one Adjudicating flags is the habit that makes AI review safe. Do it once here on sample material and the reflex carries into due diligence (b5) and every real review: let AI flag fast, decide each flag yourself, and verify it did not miss anything.
Demo 2 of 2

Draft redlines to your fallbacks ★ 8 min · build your own

Turn two decided flags into markup. Take two clauses you flagged in Demo 1, have AI draft redlines that pull them to your fallback positions, then finalize the wording yourself - the model drafts the change, you own the ask.

Pick two flags you decided to negotiate in Demo 1 - say a one-way indemnity and a missing liability cap.

For each, give AI the current clause and your fallback, and ask it to draft a redline that pulls the term to your fallback while keeping the counterparty's structure where it can.

Ask for a one-line rationale per redline - the reason you will give in negotiation, and a check that the change is justified.

Finalize each redline yourself: the wording, how hard you open, and the fallback you will settle at. You own the markup that goes back to the other side. You will reuse this reflex in b6 and refine the guardrails in b10.

★ The redline-to-fallback promptDraft a redline for this clause to move it to our fallback position. Current clause: [paste]. Our fallback (the least we will accept): [paste]. Rules: pull the term to our fallback; keep the counterparty's structure where you can; do not add terms we did not ask for; mark every change. Output: the redlined clause (changes marked), then a one-line rationale I can use in negotiation. I will finalize the wording and the ask.
Real world

The surgical redline that closed faster. A team let AI draft two redlines to their fallbacks instead of rewriting the counterparty's clauses wholesale. Each change was small, marked, and justified in a line - the other side accepted both in one pass, because nothing looked like a power grab. The lawyer had decided what to fight for and finalized every word; the AI just drafted the markup to the fallback. Fast triage, surgical asks, lawyer's judgment on top.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Finish the sample review if you did not complete it live. Adjudicating each flag yourself is the skill no summary replaces.
  • Run the same contract twice: once with no playbook, once against your playbook. Compare how much more actionable the playbook flags are.
  • Draft redlines to your fallbacks on two flagged clauses, then finalize the wording. Notice where your judgment overrode the model's draft.
  • Try the triage move: feed three sample agreements and have AI rank them by risk. Confirm the ranking matches your own read.
  • Confirm which vetted tool you are cleared to use for real client contracts. Review-along stays on sample material until that is settled.
