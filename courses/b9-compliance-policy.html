Compliance & policy work: draft grounded in the actual rule, not the model's memory

Compliance work is where AI is most tempting and most dangerous: it will happily summarize a regulation and draft a policy from what it half-remembers, and the half-remembered version is often wrong or out of date. Tonight you do it the safe way - draft policy and run gap-checks grounded in the actual regulation you supply, then verify every line against the source. AI surfaces; you confirm; you own the compliance conclusion.

Part 0

Where we are

You have built research, drafting, review, diligence, memos, client comms, and litigation support. Compliance and policy work pulls them together - and adds a sharp new risk: the rule itself. An AI asked "what does regulation X require?" will answer fluently from memory, and that memory may be wrong, incomplete, or a version ago. The discipline tonight is grounding: never let AI draft policy or summarize an obligation from its own memory - feed it the actual text and verify against the source. This is education for legal professionals, not legal advice, and rules vary by jurisdiction - verify against your regulator and your bar.

Part 1 · covers the compliance-drafting canon

Draft policy grounded in the actual rules 6 min live

AI is genuinely fast at policies, procedures, and change summaries - but only when it is grounded in the regulation you supply, not the version it half-remembers. The move is always the same: paste the actual text, ask AI to draft or summarize from that text alone, then verify every clause back against the source. AI produces the draft; the lawyer verifies against the rule and owns it.

LiveGrounding is the whole job - paste the rule, do not trust the memory3 min▶

The failure mode in compliance is quieter than a fabricated case, and just as damaging: AI writes a confident, professional policy based on a regulation it remembers slightly wrong, or as it stood two amendments ago. Nobody notices until an auditor does. The fix is grounding - give the model the actual regulatory text and instruct it to draft only from what you supplied.

  • Supply the text, always. Paste the actual regulation, standard, or obligation. "Draft from this text only - do not add requirements from memory" is the core instruction.
  • Ask it to cite back. Have the draft point each policy statement to the specific clause it came from, so verification is a lookup, not a re-read of everything.
  • Verify against the source. Read each drafted obligation against the clause it claims to implement. The draft is a strong first pass; the check is where you earn it.
★ Try it now - grounded policy draftHere is the actual regulatory text: [paste the excerpt]. Draft a short internal policy that implements ONLY what this text requires. Do not add any requirement that is not in the text. For each policy statement, cite the specific clause it comes from. Flag anything the text leaves ambiguous.
Real world

The policy built on a remembered rule. A team asked AI to "write a data-retention policy per the regulation" with no text supplied. The draft was polished and cited a retention period the model invented from a similar rule in another jurisdiction. It sat in the handbook for months. Grounding it in the actual clause would have taken one paste and caught it on line one.

Part 2 · covers regulatory tracking + gap analysis

Regulatory tracking + gap checks 6 min live

Two ongoing compliance jobs are natural fits for grounded AI: keeping up with regulatory change, and checking whether your policies actually cover your obligations. In both, AI does the surfacing - the reading-fast, the mapping, the "here are the likely gaps" - and the lawyer confirms every item against the primary text before it becomes a finding.

LiveThe gap-check pattern - task then the check3 min▶

A compliance gap-check is a mapping problem: for each obligation, does a policy or control cover it, and is the coverage adequate? AI is fast at the first pass and the lawyer confirms each result - because "covered" is a legal judgment, not a keyword match.

The task AI runsThe check the lawyer owns
List every obligation in the supplied regulationConfirm the list is complete against the primary text
Match each obligation to a policy or controlConfirm the match is real, not a loose keyword overlap
Flag obligations with no matching control (gaps)Confirm each flagged gap is genuine, not a wording mismatch
Rate coverage adequacy per obligationExercise the legal judgment on "adequate" - AI cannot
Draft remediation notes for each gapOwn the remediation plan and its priority
AI surfaces, the lawyer confirms Treat every gap AI flags as a candidate to check, and every "covered" as a claim to test. The value you add is confirming which gaps are real and which coverage is genuinely adequate - that judgment is the compliance conclusion, and it is yours.
Self-studyRegulatory tracking without trusting the memory2 min read▶
  • Summarize the actual update text. Paste the amendment or guidance; ask for a plain summary of what changed - then verify it against the primary text before you act on it.
  • Confirm currency. Ask AI when a rule applies and you may get last year's version. Check the effective date and the current consolidated text every time.
  • Map change to impact. Have AI list which of your policies or controls the change touches - as leads to review, not a settled impact assessment.
  • Keep the audit trail. Note what you asked, what AI surfaced, and what you confirmed. Compliance work has to be defensible after the fact.
Part 3 · the duty that runs through compliance work

Grounding + verification 4 min live

Compliance has its own version of the verify-and-own rail, sharpened by two facts: AI summaries of regulations are often subtly wrong, and regulations change. Never rely on either without checking the primary text - and the compliance conclusion is always yours.

LiveNever rely on an AI summary of a regulation2 min▶

An AI summary of a rule is a reading aid, not the rule. Summaries compress, and compression in regulation drops exceptions, thresholds, and defined terms - exactly the parts that decide compliance. Read the primary text before you draft policy, map a control, or advise on an obligation.

  • The summary hides the exceptions. "The rule requires X" often omits "unless Y", and Y is where the client sits. Only the primary text tells you.
  • Regulations change - confirm currency. The model's training has a cutoff and rules move past it. Check the effective date and the current consolidated version.
  • The lawyer owns the conclusion. "AI said the policy covers it" is not a compliance opinion. You confirm coverage against the text, and you own the finding.
Say it out loud once "Ground it in the actual rule, verify the summary against the primary text, and I own the compliance conclusion." That sentence is the spine of every compliance workflow you will build.
Self-studyThe three grounding questions - before any compliance output ships1 min read▶
  • Did I supply the actual text? If AI drafted from memory, start over with the real regulation pasted in.
  • Is it current? Confirm the effective date and that no amendment supersedes what you used.
  • Did I verify and do I own it? Every drafted obligation checked against its clause, every gap confirmed, the conclusion signed by a lawyer.

Three questions, every time. They are cheaper than one wrong policy in a handbook or one missed obligation in an audit.

Draft a policy from a supplied regulation excerpt ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Grounding in action. You supply a short regulation excerpt, AI drafts a policy or procedure from that text alone, and you verify every drafted obligation back against the source - the exact loop from Part 1.

Take a short, real regulation or standard excerpt (nothing client-confidential in a consumer tool). Paste the actual text into the prompt.

Instruct AI to draft a short internal policy or procedure that implements only what the text requires - no requirements added from memory.

Require a clause citation on each policy statement, so you can trace every line back to the source.

Verify line by line: does each drafted obligation actually appear in the excerpt, and does the excerpt say what the draft claims? Delete or fix anything that does not map.

Confirm currency and note the effective date. You now own a policy grounded in the real rule, not the model's memory.

Demo 2 of 2

Run a compliance gap-check ★ 8 min · build your own

The mapping problem from Part 2, run for real. You give AI a sample policy and a supplied list of obligations, it flags likely gaps, and you confirm each one against the obligation text - because "covered" is your judgment, not the model's.

Supply two things: a sample policy, and the actual list of obligations from a regulation you paste (not from memory).

Ask AI to map each obligation to the part of the policy that covers it, and to flag any obligation with no matching coverage.

For each flagged gap, confirm it against the obligation text - is it a genuine gap, or a wording mismatch where coverage exists under a different label?

For each "covered", test the match: is the coverage real and adequate, or a loose keyword overlap? Downgrade anything that does not hold.

Write the confirmed gap list with a remediation note per gap. You own the finding - AI surfaced it, you confirmed it.

★ The gap-check promptHere is a sample policy: [paste]. Here are the actual obligations: [paste the list]. For each obligation: 1) Point to the exact part of the policy that covers it, or mark it a GAP. 2) For covered items, note whether the coverage looks adequate or partial. 3) List every GAP separately at the end with a one-line remediation suggestion. Do not assume coverage from wording alone - flag anything uncertain. I will confirm each gap and each match against the obligation text myself.
Real world

The gap the keyword match hid. An AI gap-check marked an obligation "covered" because the policy used the same term - but the policy applied it to a different process, so the obligation was actually unmet. A lawyer confirming the match against the obligation text caught it; the keyword overlap alone would have shipped a false clean bill. AI surfaced the candidate; the lawyer's confirmation was the compliance conclusion.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Draft a short policy from a real regulation excerpt you supply, then verify every line against the source. Note how many statements needed a fix - that is the grounding lesson.
  • Run a gap-check of one of your existing policies against the actual obligations. Confirm each flagged gap yourself before treating it as real.
  • Ask AI to summarize a recent regulatory change, then read the primary amendment text and mark what the summary dropped or got wrong.
  • Confirm the currency of one rule you rely on - effective date and current consolidated version - and note whether AI had it right.
  • Optional: write your one-line grounding rule ("no compliance draft without the actual text pasted in") and pin it above your desk.
