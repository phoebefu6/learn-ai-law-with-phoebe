Legal research with AI: grounded, cited, verified

Legal research was AI's first great use in law and its first public disaster. This session gives you the method that keeps you on the right side of that line: ground the AI in real authority, demand pinpoint citations, set the jurisdiction, and verify everything - then drive a live simulator where you watch a fabricated case appear, get caught, and give way to a defensible answer.

Part 0

Grounding is the whole game

Ask a general AI "what is the law on X" and it will answer from the average of everything it read - confidently, and sometimes with cases that do not exist. Ask it to reason only over authority you supply, cite pinpoints, respect a jurisdiction, and flag anything it cannot ground - and it becomes a genuine research accelerator. Same tool, opposite risk. The difference is grounding plus verification, and both are on you. (Reminder: this is education, not legal advice; your jurisdiction's rules control.)

Part 1 · covers GC AI legal research + the hallucination canon

Grounded, cited, and verified 6 min live

Reliable AI research follows a chain: start from real authority, tie every proposition to a pinpoint citation, respect the governing jurisdiction, and verify each cite against primary law before you rely on it. Skip any link and you are back to confident fiction.

LiveBring the authority to the AI, not the other way around3 min▶

The single biggest shift: stop asking AI to recall the law, and start asking it to reason over law you provide. Retrieval-grounded tools do this by searching a real database first; with a general tool you do it by pasting the actual statute or cases. Either way, the model works from real text, not its memory.

  • Provide the source. Give it the statute, the cases, the clause - or use a tool that retrieves them from a real database - so it has something true to reason over.
  • Constrain it. "Use only the authorities provided", "cite pinpoints", "say 'not in the provided law' rather than guessing" - the constraints that stop invention.
  • Then verify. Even grounded, pull and read each cited authority yourself before relying on it. Grounding lowers the risk; verification closes it.
★ Try it now - grounded, not from memoryHere is the statute and two cases [paste real, non-confidential text]. Using ONLY these authorities, answer: [precise question]. Cite pinpoints to the provided text. If the authorities do not resolve it, say "not resolved by the provided law" - do not add outside cases.
Real world

Same tool, two outcomes. One associate asked a chatbot "what cases support rescission for mistake?" and got three fabrications. Another pasted the actual controlling cases and asked the tool to apply them to the facts - and got a clean, verifiable first-pass analysis in minutes. The tool did not change. The grounding did.

Self-studyVerification is the non-negotiable step2 min read▶

For every authority an AI surfaces, run the three checks before it informs your work: does the case exist, does it hold what is claimed, and is it still good law in this jurisdiction. Legal-specific tools with real citations make this faster, but the duty is identical. A citation you have not personally confirmed is a citation you cannot rely on or file.

CheckWhat it prevents
Does the case exist?Filing a fabricated citation (sanctions)
Does it hold what is claimed?Miscited authority for a proposition it never stood for
Is it still good law here?Relying on overruled or out-of-jurisdiction authority
Part 2 · the grounded-research simulator

Drive it yourself: watch fiction turn into a defensible draft ★ 8 min · everyone plays

Here is the whole lesson in one control panel. The AI is answering a legal-research query. With everything off, it confidently cites a case that does not exist - the exact 2023 trap. Turn on "verify against primary law" and watch it get caught; add the source law, pinpoint cites, and jurisdiction and watch it become a draft a lawyer could actually build on. Notice what the levers are: grounding and verification, not a bigger model.

What the simulator is teaching Start with everything off: a fabricated case, stated with total confidence - never file it. Turn on verification alone and it catches the fabrication and tells you that you have no supported answer yet. Provide the real authority, pinpoint cites, and jurisdiction and the answer becomes reliable. Grounding plus verification did that, and the lawyer still owns the result.

Run a grounded research query for real ★ 12 min · everyone builds

Now do it on a real (non-confidential) question, using the full chain from the simulator.

Pick a precise legal question and gather the real authority - statute and a couple of cases (public, non-confidential), or use a retrieval-grounded legal tool.

Prompt the AI to reason only over that authority, cite pinpoints, set the jurisdiction, and flag anything it cannot ground.

Verify: pull and read each cited authority. Confirm it exists, holds what is claimed, and is still good law here.

Write your answer from what survived verification. The AI accelerated the reasoning; you confirmed the law and own the conclusion.

Save the prompt as your grounded-research template. Every question now starts from authority, not from the model's memory.

★ The grounded-research promptJurisdiction: [X]. Question (one issue): [precise question]. Authorities to use (use ONLY these): [paste statute + cases, or "the retrieved results"]. Instructions: apply only the provided authorities to the question; cite pinpoints (section / paragraph); note where authorities conflict; and if they do not resolve the question, say "not resolved by the provided law" - do NOT introduce outside cases. Flag any proposition you cannot tie to the provided text. I will verify every citation.

Build your verification checklist ★ 8 min · build your own

Turn the duty into a repeatable gate you run before any AI research informs your work.

Write the three checks as a short checklist: exists, holds-what-is-claimed, still-good-law-here.

Add a confidentiality check (was this run in a vetted tool for the data involved) and an ownership line (a named lawyer confirmed each cite).

Make it the last step before any AI-assisted research is relied on, cited, or filed.

Run it once on the Demo 1 output to prove the habit.

Real world

The checklist that became muscle memory. A litigation team put a five-line verification checklist at the top of every research doc. Within a month it was automatic, and an AI-suggested case that had been quietly overruled got caught before it reached a brief. The habit cost seconds; the miss it prevented could have cost the motion.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · In the simulator, turning on "verify against primary law" with no sources does what?

Verification is the safety net. It catches the invented citation even before you supply real authority - which is exactly why it is a duty, not an optional step.

2 · What is the core shift for reliable AI legal research?

Grounding the model in real authority (supplied or retrieved) plus verification is what keeps citations real. No tool - legal-specific included - is zero-risk.

3 · Before relying on an AI-surfaced case, you confirm...

The three verification checks. A citation you have not personally confirmed on all three cannot be relied on or filed - and you own that check.
