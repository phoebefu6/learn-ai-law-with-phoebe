Litigation support: AI scaffolds, the lawyer owns the strategy

Litigation buries you in documents, dates, and testimony. AI is built for exactly that grind: triaging discovery, building chronologies, drafting deposition question outlines, and analyzing case and witness materials at speed. What it cannot do is decide the theory of the case. This session uses AI as scaffolding while the lawyer owns strategy and verifies every fact and cite against the record. By the end you can move faster through the litigation grind without handing over a single judgment call.

Part 1 · covers the litigation-tech canon - AI as scaffolding

AI as litigation scaffolding 6 min live

Think of AI as the scaffolding around the case, not the case itself. It triages discovery, builds chronologies, drafts deposition question outlines, and analyzes case and witness materials - fast, tireless, and good at structure. The lawyer owns the strategy and every conclusion, and verifies each fact against the record before it counts.

LiveScaffolding, not the building3 min▶

The scaffolding metaphor holds all the way through litigation. AI puts up the structure - it reads thousands of pages, pulls out dates and actors, orders events, and drafts question outlines. The building - the theory of the case, which witness to press, which document wins the motion - is the lawyer's. Fast assembly is real leverage, but it is only leverage if you verify what was assembled.

  • It reads at a scale you cannot. AI can triage a discovery set overnight and surface what looks relevant - a starting point for your review, not a substitute for it.
  • It structures well, decides nothing. A clean chronology or outline is genuinely useful; the strategic weight of each entry is your call.
  • Every fact traces to the record. An AI-asserted date or quote is a lead until you have pulled the source document. In litigation, an unverified fact is a liability.
Real world

The chronology that saved a week - after it was checked. A team fed a synthetic discovery set to AI and had a first-pass chronology in minutes instead of days. Then an associate traced every entry to its source document and caught two dates the AI had transposed. The scaffolding saved the week; the verification saved the argument that rested on those dates.

Self-studyWhere AI helps across a litigation matter2 min read▶
  • Discovery triage - first-pass relevance and privilege sorting across a large document set, for a human to review.
  • Chronology-building - extract dates, actors, and events into an ordered timeline you verify.
  • Summarization - condense long documents, transcripts, or statements into briefs you check against the source.
  • Deposition prep - draft question outlines and flag inconsistencies in witness materials as leads.
  • Witness-statement analysis - surface gaps and contradictions to investigate, never conclusions to rely on.
Part 2 · covers discovery + deposition prep

Discovery + deposition prep 5 min live

Two of litigation's heaviest lifts are where AI helps most: sifting discovery and preparing to depose. It can triage and summarize an e-discovery set, build the chronology, draft deposition outlines, and analyze witness statements. Each pairs with a human check - and every fact gets verified against the record, on vetted tools only.

Self-studyDiscovery, privilege, and confidentiality2 min read▶
  • Case documents are confidential. Discovery material and witness statements go into vetted tools with confidentiality terms - never a consumer chatbot.
  • Privilege review stays human-supervised. AI can flag likely-privileged material, but a wrong call risks waiver. The lawyer confirms every privilege designation.
  • Inconsistencies are leads. An AI-spotted contradiction is a starting point for your questioning, not a fact to assert until you have checked the underlying statements.
  • The record governs. Anything you rely on, cite, or put to a witness must trace to the actual document - not the summary.
Part 3 · the rail for this session

Strategy stays human 5 min live

AI drafts the scaffolding fast - that is real, and it is a genuine time saving. But the theory of the case, the strategy, and the judgment calls are the lawyer's, and they do not delegate. And every fact and cite you rely on gets verified against the record. Scaffolding accelerates the work; it never authors the argument.

LiveWhy the judgment cannot be handed over3 min▶

A chronology does not tell you which two events win the motion. An outline does not tell you which question breaks the witness. AI orders the facts; you decide what they mean and how to use them. That is the part that is legal work, and it stays with a lawyer who is accountable for it - and who has checked that the underlying facts are real.

  • Theory of the case is yours. The narrative that ties the facts together is judgment, not extraction. AI cannot supply it and should not be trusted to.
  • Strategy is yours. What to emphasise, what to concede, when to depose, what to hold for trial - all lawyer calls.
  • Verify every fact and cite against the record. The same rail that runs through the whole course is sharpest here: an unverified fact in litigation can lose the case, or draw sanctions if it is a fabricated cite.
Say it out loud once "AI scaffolds the litigation; I own the strategy and I verify every fact against the record." That sentence is the spine of this session.
Self-studyThe line between assembly and argument2 min read▶
  • Assembly (AI helps): reading, extracting, ordering, summarizing, drafting outlines - the volume work.
  • Argument (lawyer owns): theory of the case, strategy, which facts matter, how to question, what to file.
  • Verification (lawyer always): every fact traced to the record, every cite confirmed - no exceptions in litigation.
  • Confidentiality (lawyer always): case material in vetted tools only; privilege calls human-confirmed.
  • Remember: this is education for legal professionals, not legal advice; your bar's rules control.
Demo 1 of 2

Build a case chronology from a supplied set ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Take a small supplied (synthetic) document set and have AI build a chronology - then verify every entry against the record as the lawyer. This is the scaffolding-plus-verification loop in one build.

Use the supplied synthetic document set - a handful of dated items: emails, a memo, a statement. Nothing from a live matter.

Ask the AI to build a chronology: date, event, actor, and the source document for each entry, in order.

Verify against the record. Open each source and confirm the date, the event, and the quote. Watch for transposed dates and events attributed to the wrong document.

Mark each entry verified or corrected. An entry AI could not source is a gap to investigate, not a fact to keep.

Note how much faster the assembly was - and that the verification is what makes the chronology usable in the matter.

The entry to distrust (unverified)March 3 - defendant admits the defect in an email to the plaintiff. [Source: Exhibit 4.] Looks solid - but Exhibit 4 is dated March 30, and the "admission" is the AI's paraphrase, not a quote. Trace it before you rely on it.
★ The chronology promptBuild a case chronology from the documents I supply. For each event give: date, what happened, who was involved, and the exact source document (and page/line if available). Order chronologically. Quote the source rather than paraphrasing where a quote matters. Flag any event you inferred rather than found stated, and any date you were unsure of. This is a draft for a lawyer to verify against the record - every entry must trace to a source.
★ You just built A case chronology assembled by AI and verified entry-by-entry against the record - fast scaffolding, every fact checked, the strategy still yours.
Demo 2 of 2

Draft a deposition question outline ★ 8 min · build your own

Have AI draft a deposition question outline from the supplied materials, then refine it for strategy - the order, the pressure points, and what to hold back. AI supplies the scaffold; you supply the plan.

Using the supplied synthetic materials, ask the AI for a deposition question outline covering the key topics and any inconsistencies it noticed in the statements.

Read it as strategy, not a script. AI groups questions by topic; you decide the order, where to press, and where to leave room.

Treat every flagged inconsistency as a lead. Confirm it against the actual statement before you build a line of questioning on it.

Refine for the plan: what to establish early, what to hold for impeachment, what to keep out of the room. That sequencing is the lawyer's judgment.

★ The deposition-outline promptDraft a deposition question outline from the supplied materials. - Group questions by topic and cover the key events in the chronology. - Flag any inconsistencies between statements as leads for me to verify, not conclusions. - Keep questions open where I want the witness to talk, tighter where I want to pin a fact. - Note which questions depend on a document so I can have it ready. This is a draft for a lawyer to refine for strategy and verify against the record.
Real world

The outline that got the sequence right - after the lawyer resequenced it. AI produced a thorough, topic-ordered set of deposition questions in a minute. The lawyer kept the coverage, but moved the impeachment questions to the end, held back one document, and cut a line that would have tipped the theory early. The AI gave breadth; the strategy - the order and the restraint - was entirely human.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Finish the chronology build if you did not complete it live, and trace every entry to its source. The verification is the lesson, not the assembly.
  • Take a small synthetic or non-confidential document set and produce both a chronology and a deposition outline. Time the assembly versus the verification.
  • On the outline, resequence for strategy: mark what you would establish early, hold for impeachment, or keep out of the room.
  • Pick one AI-flagged inconsistency and confirm it against the underlying statement. Note whether it held up.
  • Optional: write your own one-line litigation rule - "AI scaffolds, I own the strategy and verify every fact against the record" - for your matter checklist.
